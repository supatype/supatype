Privacy

What www.supatype.com collects, why, and how to have it removed. The Supatype software you install and run yourself sends us nothing.

Last updated 28 October 2026.

Who we are

Supatype Ltd runs this site and decides what happens to the data on this page. Email hello@supatype.com about anything here.

Usage analytics

We use PostHog, hosted in the EU, to count visits and see which pages and buttons people use. We record the page, the site that sent you, your browser and device type, and clicks such as Get started or copying a command.

  • PostHog runs in cookieless mode: it stores nothing in your browser. It counts visitors with a hash it makes on its own servers that changes every day, so we cannot follow you from one day to the next.
  • We have set PostHog not to keep your IP address, and we do not record sessions.
  • We use this to make the site better. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in knowing how the site is used.

Cloud waiting list and call requests

If you join the waiting list or ask us to book a call, we store your email address in PostHog with the date, which form you used, and anything you wrote in the optional note.

  • We use a waiting list address only to tell you about Supatype Cloud: when it opens, and anything you need to know before then. We use a call request only to arrange and hold that call.
  • We do not sell it or share it with anyone else.
  • We keep a waiting list address until Cloud opens and you have had our launch email, and a call request for a year after the call, or until you ask us to remove it.
  • The legal basis is your consent. You can withdraw it at any time by emailing us.

Other services this site uses

  • GitHub Pages hosts the site, its fonts included, and keeps standard server logs.
  • Links to GitHub, Discord, ChatGPT and Claude take you to those sites, under their own privacy policies.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct or delete what we hold about you, or object to how we use it. Email hello@supatype.com and we will reply within one month.

If you are unhappy with our answer, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.
